# Product behavior

Appa helps one person turn an intention into a manageable next action, and return after missed work without manually repairing the calendar.

## Agreed behavior

- Selecting an event in Day, Week or Month opens a floating task card beside it, above the calendar without a backdrop or modal focus trap. The card shows its goal, title, date, 24-hour time range and duration, with Edit and Mark as complete actions and later pending steps from the same goal. Clicking a later step opens it in the card. The card fits within the viewport; outside clicks, Close and Escape dismiss it, and calendar navigation closes it. Shared activities retain their choice controls and completion rules. Completed events show their own completed task.
- Edit switches the same card to name, date and start/end-time fields with Cancel and Update. Duration is derived from the time range (5–60 minutes); midnight can be entered as 00:00. Cancel or Escape in edit mode returns to the saved details. Changes save locally and support Undo. Dates must be today or later; times must fit available hours without overlaps, and unfinished prerequisites must stay on earlier dates than their dependent steps. Move later steps first when moving a prerequisite past them. Moving one shared alternative gives it its own slot and retains other alternatives and choices where possible; an edit is rejected if the remaining reservation would overlap. Recurring edits apply only to that occurrence, preserving repeat identity and future templates.

- English interface, Day, Week and Month views with a shared selected date and a mini month navigator. Today returns to the current date in the chosen view. Desktop starts empty and uses the computer's timezone.
- Month scrolls vertically through earlier and later month grids, with fixed weekday labels. The main month heading and mini calendar follow the month at the viewport's center; scrolling does not change the selected date. Today, header arrows and mini-calendar date selections jump to the requested month, including repeated jumps to the same month. A moving seven-month render window preserves the scroll position as months are added and removed.
- Selected dates in Month view, the Week header and the mini calendar use red circular highlights with white text.
- Week always shows all seven days, starting on the preferred weekday (Monday by default), with no five/seven-day toggle. The displayed timeline always spans 00:00–24:00; this full-day range does not change scheduling availability.
- Day and Week rows expand when needed to keep consecutive short steps readable without overlapping their cards. Empty timelines and longer appointments use the compact baseline spacing.
- Week shows a red current-time line with a 24-hour HH:mm time label in the gutter, a solid segment and dot on today, and lighter segments on other visible days. Day uses the same 24-hour current-time label. It follows the calendar's timezone, updates on each minute and when returning to the app, and is hidden in weeks without today.
- Day and Week open at the current time in the calendar's timezone, with roughly one hour of context above it, instead of focusing on scheduled tasks. Entering either timeline or navigating dates uses the same time position. Today returns to the current date and repositions the timeline even within the same day or week. Clock updates and task edits preserve manual scrolling.
- Day displays a full 00:00–24:00 timeline on the left and the selected day's task list on the right. Both show the same scheduled slots and open the same task details, choices and completion controls. The panels scroll independently; narrow screens stack the task list below the timeline. The red current-time indicator appears only on today.
- Day has a seven-day date row above both panels, starting on the preferred weekday and showing the week containing the selected date. Selecting a date updates the timeline, task list and mini calendar together. The row stays visible as either panel scrolls; the selected date is filled, and today uses the red accent.
- Day's task-list width is adjustable with the divider. Drag left to widen it or right to narrow it; focused Left/Right arrows adjust by 10 pixels (Shift: 40), Home/End select the available limits, and double-click restores the 320-pixel default. The list stays between 240 and 640 pixels, preserving at least 240 pixels for the timeline. A narrower window temporarily clamps the width without discarding the saved preference. Width is saved locally, separately from calendar data and backups. On stacked narrow screens the divider is hidden.
- Select a calendar task to open its floating task card, edit fields, shared choices and completion controls. The floating goal input opens the existing planner with the intention prefilled; approval is still required before scheduling.
- Any chosen number of active goals. Later keeps accepted plans without scheduling them. Activating a saved goal preserves its identity and existing progress and keeps other active goals in place.
- AI proposals are editable and require approval before scheduling. Manual planning works without an AI account. Aim at 5–25 minute tasks; the editor accepts up to 60 minutes when the daily window permits.
- Scheduling uses available hours, initially 18:00–21:00 and adjustable, with no separate daily time limit. The user removed the daily budget on 2026-10-13. Older saved calendars and backups remain supported; their former budget field is discarded when loaded.
- Settings has four sections: User, Calendar settings, Goal settings and Backup. A left menu opens one content panel at a time, starting on User. Calendar and Goal edits are retained while switching sections; Cancel discards the draft and Save settings applies it. User and Backup show their own actions with Done to close. A pending ChatGPT sign-in remains available when navigating away and back. User provides ChatGPT sign-in, the active account and Sign out. Desktop remembers the session securely across restarts and renews access automatically. The planner shows Use your ChatGPT plan only while disconnected; a connected user sees the model selector without the sign-in panel. Signing in from either place updates the same account, and returning from the system browser checks the connection automatically. Calendar settings includes timezone, interface language, week numbers, first weekday and public holidays. Goal settings retains available hours. Backup retains validated export/import. Display preferences are saved with the calendar, included in backups and reversible with Undo; changing display preferences alone preserves scheduled tasks and choices exactly.
- Public holidays default from the calendar timezone (including Australian states where identifiable), with country/region overrides and None. Unknown or ambiguous regional timezones fall back to national coverage or None. Day, Week, Month and the mini calendar show the selected calendar's local holiday dates, including substitute dates and partial-day hours, independently of the display timezone. Holidays are reference labels and do not block scheduling. Bundled date-holidays 3.37.0 data works offline; local show days and one-off changes may be absent. License notices ship with the desktop app.
- Week-start applies consistently to Day, Week, Month and the mini calendar. Week numbers are optional in all four; each visible week's label uses the ISO week number of its Thursday. Scheduling recurrence remains based on its existing Monday cadence, independently of display preferences.
- English is the only enabled interface language. A shared interface language provider and English message catalog cover calendar controls, planning/settings/goal dialogs, notices and accessibility labels. `npm run extract:messages` refreshes the English catalog; named placeholders keep variable content separate. Register complete translations in `src/i18n.ts` before enabling another language, and verify date formatting, holiday names and dynamic service errors in that locale. Legacy saved data receives English, Monday, visible week numbers and automatic holidays without losing progress.
- Prerequisites constrain actionable work and move forward in dependency order. Independent work remains in place unless involved in a scheduling conflict.
- Shared slots contain alternatives. Reserve their maximum duration until selection, then the selected duration. Selection alone changes no completion state.
- Completing a selected shared task skips unchosen recurring occurrences in that slot; project alternatives remain unfinished, and future repeats keep their cadence.
- Catch-up retains one pending overdue occurrence per recurring activity, preserves unfinished projects, and shows recovery details with Undo. Undo applies to the latest committed change in the current page session.
- Calendar data and draft proposals are saved locally, with validated JSON export/import. Cross-device synchronization, repeated-postponement suggestions, deadline warnings and automatic updates are deferred.
- Saved calendars have no fixed lifetime goal/task count limit. Imports accept JSON files up to 20 MB. If saving fails, the recovery export includes the current unsaved progress; a failed initial load instead preserves the original saved file for recovery.
- Adding or recovering work and changing available hours preserve explicit shared-slot choices. A chosen group moves to a later feasible day instead of merging away its selection; accepted settings must pass the same capacity checks as reload. New alternatives must fit the full reservation when no choice exists. Active recurring templates must fit the available hours, including when no upcoming occurrences exist; automatic scheduling errors leave the calendar usable.
- Reload and backup import determine the current day from the saved calendar's validated timezone. Historical completed work keeps its original hours when availability changes on a later local day, including when importing into a workspace in another timezone.
- Disconnect invalidates pending sign-in, token renewal and model-loading results immediately. The model shown and submitted always comes from the currently connected account's catalog.

Active goals expose Edit goal from their details. Users can rename a goal, add/remove pending steps, change step titles, durations and prerequisites, and switch between a project and recurring activity. Recurring goals support activity templates and 1–7 sessions per week. Completed steps stay read-only. Renaming preserves schedules; recurrence changes rebuild only that goal's upcoming sessions. Stopping recurrence replaces upcoming repeats with the displayed project steps. Invalid edits leave saved data intact, and successful saves persist locally and support Undo in the current session.

## Main modules

`src/types.ts` defines the shared goal, task, plan, calendar and API contracts. `src/calendar.ts` owns scheduling, recurrence, recovery, validation and reversible changes. `src/App.tsx` coordinates the calendar views; `src/CalendarTimeline.tsx` supplies the shared Day and Week timeline; `src/CalendarViews.tsx` supplies the month grid, mini calendar and date helpers; `src/Dialogs.tsx` owns planning, settings and goal details. `server/chatgpt.ts` supplies the reusable local OAuth/inference service and browser-development adapter. `desktop/` supplies Electron startup, its private loopback server, and external-link policy.

The visual baseline is the calendar reference supplied on 2026-10-14: white and soft grey surfaces, a narrow left rail, compact task cards, restrained type, and a floating composer with a red circular action. Appa keeps its own identity and real planning controls. On narrow screens, the rail becomes goal/settings buttons and the week can scroll horizontally. Day and Month views fit the screen.

## Calendar surface layout — 2026-10-15

The user approved a continuous soft grey base behind the menu and outer canvas, with the calendar on a single white rounded panel. The month/week title and all view/navigation controls belong inside that panel. The applied layout uses an 8-pixel outer margin, reduced from the mockup at the user’s request, and 4 pixels on narrow screens. Day, Week and Month share this surface. Preserve Appa’s existing typography, icons, data and behavior when maintaining this layout.

## Compact calendar content — 2026-10-15

The user requested smaller content while retaining the white calendar card. Keep its outer size, 8px desktop inset, 4px narrow-screen inset and sidebar width. The main header is 52px, month title 17px, week label 14px, Week date row 44px, Day date row 52px and composer 560px wide by 48px high at most desktop widths. Empty timeline hours use a 56px baseline; rows expand to reserve at least 20px per shortest step so five-minute events remain readable without overlap. Month headers and task-list spacing are also compact. These changes apply inside the calendar surface; the later task-card typography adjustment below is separate from the calendar surface sizing.

## Compact task card typography — 2026-10-15

The user supplied an event-details reference for typography proportions. The floating task card uses a 17px medium-weight title and 13px body text (approximately 1.3:1), with 12px secondary text and 11px captions. Its width is 360px, capped at viewport width minus 32px, with 18px padding (16px on narrow screens), a 3px accent rule and 34px actions. Edit fields use the same title/body scale. Preserve the existing white card, content, section order and behaviour; the reference does not change Appa to a dark theme or add meeting features.

The reusable content, control and calendar typography sets and the approved 1.3:1 title/body ratio are documented in [DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md).

## Shared typography and legibility — 2026-10-16

Button usage follows the behavior rules in [DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md) while retaining Appa's visual style. Planner Back to intention keeps the draft and exposes Continue editing plan. Remove step is labelled and supports a single Undo removal until another plan edit or planner closure; undo restores prerequisites too. Icon-only actions explain themselves on hover/focus, except the familiar Close and red composer actions explicitly excluded by the user. External links announce new-tab navigation, and destructive confirmations say Remove example goals or Replace calendar. Sign-in status uses Check connection. Calendar-level reset/import remain reversible through their existing Undo flow.

The active interface uses the shared typography roles in [DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md), retaining 17px titles and 13px body text. Settings section headings use 14px beneath the 17px dialog title; primary and secondary actions use 13px semibold text at all widths. Secondary text uses `#66666c`, selected dates use `#c82240`, and the current-time indicator uses `#c5362b` so their informative text has stronger contrast. Numeric dates, times and durations use tabular figures.

Desktop Month task names use 11px; at widths up to 600px they use 10px and reduced horizontal padding. Month labels wrap at widths up to 1050px, and rows can grow to retain the complete name. Narrow Settings shows wrapping summaries of complete timezone and holiday-calendar selections, while keeping the native fields and left menu. These changes preserve the approved calendar surface and task-card footprint.
